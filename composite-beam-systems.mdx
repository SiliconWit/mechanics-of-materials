A machine bed made of one material is a compromise: steel is stiff but heavy, and aluminum is light but bends more. Join the two and you can have both. But the usual bending formula now gives wrong stresses, because it assumes one material, and in a bonded beam the stiffer material takes more than its share. This lesson gives you the transformed section method: treat the whole beam as one material, with the stiffer part drawn wider, and the bending formula you know works again. You use it on the steel and aluminum bed of a CNC (computer-controlled) machine. #CompositeBeams #TransformedSection #CNCDesign

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. **Analyze** composite beams made of different materials using the transformed section method
2. **Locate** the neutral axis in composite beam cross-sections
3. **Calculate** bending stresses in each material of a composite system
4. **Judge** how much each material adds to the strength and stiffness of the beam

## Real-World System Problem: CNC machine bed structure

<InArticleAd />


The bed of a CNC machine carries the cutting forces and must bend very little, or the cut is not accurate. A common design casts aluminum around a steel I-beam (a section shaped like the letter I: two horizontal flanges joined by a vertical web). The aluminum gives the mounting surfaces and keeps the weight low; the steel adds stiffness.

```text
   ◄────────── 300 mm ──────────►
   ┌─────────────────────────────┐ ▲
   │  aluminum casting           │ │
   │      ┌───────────┐          │ │
   │      └────┐ ┌────┘ steel    │ │
   │           │ │      I-beam   │ 250 mm
   │           │ │      150 wide │ │
   │      ┌────┘ └────┐ 200 tall │ │
   │      └───────────┘          │ │
   │                             │ │
   └─────────────────────────────┘ ▼
```

> **Engineering Question:** When this bed bends, how much stress does each material carry, and is each material safe?

## Fundamental Theory: Composite beam mechanics

<InArticleAd />


### Same strain, different stress

Press a stiff spring and a soft spring side by side through the same distance: the stiff one pushes back harder. Two bonded materials in a beam do the same. The bond makes them stretch together, so at the bond line the strain (stretch per unit length) is the same in both. Stress is strain times the elastic modulus $E$ (the stiffness of the material, GPa), so the stiffer material carries more stress.

Unlike the springs, a beam does not stretch evenly: the strain is zero at the neutral axis and grows with the distance from it. All of this needs a perfect bond; if the materials slip, they bend separately and the results below no longer hold.

### The transformed section method

The bending formula $\sigma = My/I$ is for one material, so the real section is replaced by an imaginary section of one material (the reference material) that bends the same way. A part of the other material is $n$ times as stiff, so it is drawn $n$ times as wide; its height and position do not change.

<Card title="Transformation ratio" icon="document">
$$n = \frac{E_{other}}{E_{reference}}$$

- $n$ = transformation ratio, also called the modular ratio (a ratio, so it has no unit)
- $E_{other}$ = elastic modulus of the material that is replaced (GPa)
- $E_{reference}$ = elastic modulus of the reference material (GPa)

With aluminum ($E = 70$ GPa) as the reference, steel ($E = 200$ GPa) has $n = 200/70 = 2.86$. Each steel part is drawn 2.86 times as wide, as if it were aluminum.
</Card>

### Composite beam analysis steps

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Step 1: Transform">

Choose the reference material and find $n$. Multiply the width of every part of the other material by $n$. Change widths only, never heights.

  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Step 2: Locate neutral axis">

The neutral axis passes through the centroid (the balance point) of the transformed section:

$$\bar{y} = \frac{\sum A_i y_i}{\sum A_i}$$

- $\bar{y}$ = height of the neutral axis above the bottom of the section (mm)
- $A_i$ = area of each part of the transformed section (mm²)
- $y_i$ = height of the centroid of that part above the bottom (mm)

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Step 3: Calculate properties">

Find the second moment of area $I$ of the transformed section about the neutral axis. The parallel axis theorem adds the parts:

$$I_{transformed} = \sum (I_i + A_i d_i^2)$$

- $I_i$ = second moment of area of each part about its own centroid (mm⁴)
- $d_i$ = distance from that centroid to the neutral axis (mm)

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Step 4: Find stresses">

In the reference material, the usual formula applies. In the other material, multiply by $n$ to return from the imaginary section to the real material:

$$\sigma_{reference} = \frac{My}{I_{transformed}} \qquad \sigma_{other} = n \times \frac{My}{I_{transformed}}$$

- $M$ = bending moment (N·mm)
- $y$ = distance from the neutral axis (mm)
- $\sigma$ = bending stress (MPa)

To forget the factor $n$ is the most common mistake in this method.

  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Application: CNC bed composite beam analysis

<InArticleAd />


:::note[Problem Statement]
- **Steel I-beam:** 150 mm wide and 200 mm tall, flanges 12 mm thick, web 8 mm thick, $E = 200$ GPa, $\sigma_{yield} = 250$ MPa
- **Aluminum casting:** 300 mm wide and 250 mm tall, with the I-beam at its centre, $E = 70$ GPa, $\sigma_{yield} = 140$ MPa
- **Span:** 2000 mm, simply supported (the bed rests on a support at each end)
- **Load:** $w = 50$ kN/m spread evenly over the span, 100 kN in total
- **Required safety factor:** 2.0

**Key Question:** What is the largest bending stress in the aluminum and in the steel, and does each material have a safety factor of at least 2?
:::

### Step 1: Calculate transformation ratio

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal transformation ratio calculations**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Transformation ratio**, with aluminum as the reference material:

   $$n = \frac{E_{steel}}{E_{aluminum}} = \frac{200 \text{ GPa}}{70 \text{ GPa}} = 2.86$$ ✅

2. **Transformed widths.** Multiply each steel width by $n$. The 150 mm flanges become $150 \times 2.86 = 429$ mm of aluminum, and the 8 mm web becomes $8 \times 2.86 = 23$ mm. The heights do not change. ✅

</Steps>

</details>

### Step 2: Locate neutral axis of transformed section

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal neutral axis calculations**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Area of the steel I-beam:** two flanges and the web. The web is $200 - 2 \times 12 = 176$ mm tall:

   $$A_s = 2 \times (150 \times 12) + 176 \times 8 = 3600 + 1408 = 5008 \text{ mm}^2$$ ✅

2. **Area of the aluminum**, the full rectangle less the steel:

   $$A_1 = (300 \times 250) - 5008 = 69{,}992 \text{ mm}^2$$ ✅

3. **Transformed area of the steel**, its real area times $n = 200/70$:

   $$A_2 = n A_s = \frac{200}{70} \times 5008 = 14{,}309 \text{ mm}^2$$ ✅

4. **Neutral axis.** Both parts have their centroid at mid-height, 125 mm above the bottom:

   $$\bar{y} = \frac{A_1 \bar{y}_1 + A_2 \bar{y}_2}{A_1 + A_2} = \frac{69{,}992 \times 125 + 14{,}309 \times 125}{69{,}992 + 14{,}309} = 125 \text{ mm}$$

   The section is symmetric, so the neutral axis stays at mid-height. In Problem 1 below it moves. ✅

</Steps>

</details>

### Step 3: Calculate maximum bending moment and stresses

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal bending moment and stress calculations**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Largest bending moment**, at the middle of a simply supported span with an even load, where $w = 50$ kN/m $= 50{,}000$ N/m:

   $$M_{max} = \frac{wL^2}{8} = \frac{50{,}000 \text{ N/m} \times (2 \text{ m})^2}{8} = 25{,}000 \text{ N·m} = 25 \times 10^6 \text{ N·mm}$$ ✅

2. **Second moment of area of the full rectangle:**

   $$I_{rect} = \frac{300 \times 250^3}{12} = 3.906 \times 10^8 \text{ mm}^4$$ ✅

3. **Second moment of area of the steel I-beam:** its outside rectangle less the two empty spaces beside the web, which are together $150 - 8 = 142$ mm wide and 176 mm tall:

   $$I_s = \frac{150 \times 200^3}{12} - \frac{142 \times 176^3}{12} = 3.549 \times 10^7 \text{ mm}^4$$ ✅

4. **Transformed second moment of area.** All parts share one centroid, so no parallel axis terms are needed. The aluminum is the rectangle less the steel, and the steel counts $n$ times, so $n - 1 = 1.857$ is added:

   $$I_{transformed} = (I_{rect} - I_s) + n I_s = I_{rect} + (n - 1) I_s$$

   $$I_{transformed} = 3.906 \times 10^8 + 1.857 \times 3.549 \times 10^7 = 4.57 \times 10^8 \text{ mm}^4$$ ✅

5. **Distances from the neutral axis** to the outer surface of each material: $c_{al} = 125$ mm (top or bottom of the casting) and $c_s = 100$ mm (outer face of the steel flanges). ✅

</Steps>

</details>

### Step 4: Calculate stresses and safety factors

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal stress and safety factor calculations**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Stress in the aluminum**, at its outer surface:

   $$\sigma_{aluminum} = \frac{M c_{al}}{I_{transformed}} = \frac{25 \times 10^6 \text{ N·mm} \times 125 \text{ mm}}{4.57 \times 10^8 \text{ mm}^4} = 6.8 \text{ MPa}$$ ✅

2. **Stress in the steel**, at the outer face of its flanges. Multiply by $n$ to return from aluminum to the real steel:

   $$\sigma_{steel} = n \times \frac{M c_s}{I_{transformed}} = 2.86 \times \frac{25 \times 10^6 \text{ N·mm} \times 100 \text{ mm}}{4.57 \times 10^8 \text{ mm}^4} = 15.6 \text{ MPa}$$ ✅

3. **Safety factors**, yield strength over stress. Aluminum: $140/6.8 = 20.5$. Steel: $250/15.6 = 16.0$. Both are far above the required 2.0, because a machine bed is sized to bend very little, not for strength. ✅

4. **What the steel adds.** At $y = 100$ mm the aluminum carries $5.5$ MPa and the steel beside it $15.6$ MPa, 2.86 times as much. The steel is $6.7\%$ of the area and gives $n I_s / I_{transformed} = 22\%$ of the bending stiffness. ✅

</Steps>

</details>

## Engineering problems

<InArticleAd />


### Problem 1: Steel-concrete composite beam

A steel I-beam carries a concrete floor slab that is cast on its top flange, as in many buildings.

```text
   ◄──────────── 1000 mm ────────────►
   ┌─────────────────────────────────┐ ▲
   │          concrete slab          │ │ 100 mm
   └────────────┬───────┬────────────┘ ▼
                └──┐ ┌──┘              ▲
                   │ │   steel I-beam  │ 200 mm
                ┌──┘ └──┐              │
                └───────┘              ▼
```

**Given:**
- Steel I-beam W200×36: area $A = 4580$ mm², second moment of area $I = 40.5 \times 10^6$ mm⁴, 200 mm tall, centroid 100 mm above its bottom
- Concrete slab: 1000 mm wide and 100 mm thick
- Simply supported span $L = 6000$ mm, with an even load $w = 15$ kN/m that includes the weight of the beam
- $E_{steel} = 200$ GPa, $E_{concrete} = 25$ GPa
- Perfect bond between steel and concrete

**Find:** the largest stress in the steel and in the concrete.

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal solution**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Transformation ratio**, with steel as the reference. Concrete is less stiff than steel, so $n$ is below 1 and the slab becomes narrower:

   $$n = \frac{E_{\text{concrete}}}{E_{\text{steel}}} = \frac{25}{200} = 0.125 \qquad b_t = 0.125 \times 1000 = 125\ \text{mm}$$ ✅

2. **Neutral axis**, measured from the bottom of the steel. Steel: $A_1 = 4580$ mm², centroid at $\bar{y}_1 = 100$ mm. Transformed slab: $A_2 = 125 \times 100 = 12{,}500$ mm², centroid at $\bar{y}_2 = 200 + 50 = 250$ mm:

   $$\bar{y} = \frac{A_1 \bar{y}_1 + A_2 \bar{y}_2}{A_1 + A_2} = \frac{4580 \times 100 + 12{,}500 \times 250}{4580 + 12{,}500} = 209.8\ \text{mm}$$ ✅

3. **Second moment of area of the transformed slab** about its own centroid:

   $$I_{\text{slab}} = \frac{125 \times 100^3}{12} = 10.4 \times 10^6\ \text{mm}^4$$ ✅

4. **Second moment of area of the whole section**, by the parallel axis theorem. Each part adds its own value and its area times the square of its distance from the neutral axis:

   $$I_{\text{composite}} = 40.5 \times 10^6 + 4580 \times (209.8 - 100)^2 + 10.4 \times 10^6 + 12{,}500 \times (250 - 209.8)^2$$

   $$I_{\text{composite}} = (40.5 + 55.2 + 10.4 + 20.2) \times 10^6 = 126.3 \times 10^6\ \text{mm}^4$$ ✅

5. **Largest bending moment**, at the middle of the span:

   $$M_{max} = \frac{wL^2}{8} = \frac{15 \text{ kN/m} \times (6 \text{ m})^2}{8} = 67.5\ \text{kN}\cdot\text{m} = 67.5 \times 10^6\ \text{N}\cdot\text{mm}$$ ✅

6. **Stress in the steel**, at its bottom surface (tension), $c_s = 209.8$ mm from the neutral axis:

   $$\sigma_s = \frac{M c_s}{I} = \frac{67.5 \times 10^6 \times 209.8}{126.3 \times 10^6} = 112.1\ \text{MPa}$$ ✅

7. **Stress in the concrete**, at its top surface (compression), $c_c = 300 - 209.8 = 90.2$ mm from the neutral axis. Multiply by $n$ to return to the real concrete:

   $$\sigma_c = n \times \frac{M c_c}{I} = 0.125 \times \frac{67.5 \times 10^6 \times 90.2}{126.3 \times 10^6} = 6.0\ \text{MPa}$$

   The neutral axis lies 9.8 mm inside the slab. That thin strip of concrete is in tension, and its stress is below $0.7$ MPa, so it is kept in the section here. ✅

</Steps>

</details>

### Problem 2: Aluminum-steel hybrid beam

A light beam has an upright steel web between two aluminum flanges, one on top and one below.

**Given:**
- Steel web: 200 mm tall and 8 mm thick, $E_{steel} = 200$ GPa
- Aluminum flanges: 150 mm wide and 20 mm thick, top and bottom, $E_{aluminum} = 70$ GPa
- Cantilever (fixed at one end, free at the other) of length $L = 1200$ mm
- Point load $P = 5000$ N at the free end
- Perfect bond between the materials

**Find:** the largest bending stress in the steel and in the aluminum.

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal solution**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Transformation ratio**, with steel as the reference, and the transformed flange width:

   $$n = \frac{E_{\text{aluminum}}}{E_{\text{steel}}} = \frac{70}{200} = 0.35 \qquad b_t = 150 \times 0.35 = 52.5\ \text{mm}$$ ✅

2. **Neutral axis.** The section is symmetric and $20 + 200 + 20 = 240$ mm tall, so the neutral axis is at mid-height, 120 mm from the bottom. The centroid of each flange is $120 - 10 = 110$ mm from it. ✅

3. **Second moment of area:** the web, and for each of the two flanges its own value and its area times $110^2$:

   $$I_{\text{transformed}} = \frac{8 \times 200^3}{12} + 2 \times \left[\frac{52.5 \times 20^3}{12} + 52.5 \times 20 \times 110^2\right]$$

   $$I_{\text{transformed}} = 5.33 \times 10^6 + 2 \times \left[35{,}000 + 12.7 \times 10^6\right] = 30.8 \times 10^6\ \text{mm}^4$$ ✅

4. **Largest bending moment**, at the fixed end:

   $$M_{max} = P \times L = 5000 \text{ N} \times 1200 \text{ mm} = 6 \times 10^6\ \text{N}\cdot\text{mm}$$ ✅

5. **Stress in the steel web** (the reference material). It is largest at the top and bottom edges of the web, $y = 100$ mm:

   $$\sigma_{\text{steel}} = \frac{M y}{I_{\text{transformed}}} = \frac{6 \times 10^6 \times 100}{30.8 \times 10^6} = 19.5\ \text{MPa}$$ ✅

6. **Stress in the aluminum flanges**, at the outer surface, $c = 120$ mm. Multiply by $n$:

   $$\sigma_{\text{aluminum}} = n \times \frac{M c}{I_{\text{transformed}}} = 0.35 \times \frac{6 \times 10^6 \times 120}{30.8 \times 10^6} = 8.2\ \text{MPa}$$

   The steel stress is higher although the steel is closer to the neutral axis, because steel is stiffer. ✅

</Steps>

</details>

### Problem 3: Carbon fiber-aluminum composite beam

An aluminum bar has a thin carbon fiber strip bonded to its top and to its bottom.

**Given:**
- Aluminum bar: 60 mm wide and 80 mm tall, $E_{aluminum} = 70$ GPa
- Carbon fiber strips: 60 mm wide and 3 mm thick, top and bottom, $E_{CF} = 150$ GPa
- Simply supported span $L = 800$ mm, with an even load $w = 2000$ N/m
- Perfect bond between the materials

**Find:** the largest stress in each material, and the stresses at the bond line.

<details>
<summary>**Click to reveal solution**</summary>

<Steps>

1. **Transformation ratio**, with aluminum as the reference, and the transformed strip width:

   $$n = \frac{E_{\text{CF}}}{E_{\text{Al}}} = \frac{150}{70} = 2.14 \qquad b_t = 60 \times 2.14 = 128.6\ \text{mm}$$ ✅

2. **Neutral axis.** The section is symmetric, so the neutral axis is at mid-height: 40 mm from each face of the aluminum and 43 mm from each outer surface. The centroid of each strip is $40 + 1.5 = 41.5$ mm from it. ✅

3. **Second moment of area of the aluminum:**

   $$I_{\text{aluminum}} = \frac{60 \times 80^3}{12} = 2.56 \times 10^6\ \text{mm}^4$$ ✅

4. **Second moment of area of the two transformed strips**, each with its own value and its area times $41.5^2$:

   $$I_{\text{CF}} = 2 \times \left[\frac{128.6 \times 3^3}{12} + 128.6 \times 3 \times 41.5^2\right] = 2 \times \left[289.3 + 664{,}296\right] = 1{,}329{,}000\ \text{mm}^4$$

   $$I_{\text{total}} = 2.56 \times 10^6 + 1.329 \times 10^6 = 3.889 \times 10^6\ \text{mm}^4$$ ✅

5. **Largest bending moment**, at the middle of the span:

   $$M_{max} = \frac{wL^2}{8} = \frac{2000 \text{ N/m} \times (0.8 \text{ m})^2}{8} = 160\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m} = 160 \times 10^3\ \text{N}\cdot\text{mm}$$ ✅

6. **Stress in the aluminum**, at its outer face, $y = 40$ mm:

   $$\sigma_{\text{Al}} = \frac{My}{I} = \frac{160 \times 10^3 \times 40}{3.889 \times 10^6} = 1.65\ \text{MPa}$$ ✅

7. **Stress in the carbon fiber**, at the outer surface of the beam, $c = 40 + 3 = 43$ mm. Multiply by $n$:

   $$\sigma_{\text{CF}} = n \times \frac{Mc}{I} = \frac{2.14 \times 160 \times 10^3 \times 43}{3.889 \times 10^6} = 3.79\ \text{MPa}$$ ✅

8. **Stresses at the bond line** ($y = 40$ mm). The strain is the same in both materials there, but the stress steps from $1.65$ MPa in the aluminum to $2.14 \times 1.65 = 3.53$ MPa in the carbon fiber. ✅

</Steps>

</details>

:::tip[Instrument it]
Bond a strain gauge to each material, close to the bond line on each side. The two strains are almost equal, and the stresses differ by the ratio of the moduli. Read both gauges with an [ESP32 or STM32](/education/sensor-actuator-interfacing-stm32/) and you can [trend the two stresses on siliconwit.io](https://siliconwit.io). If the two strains begin to differ, the bond is failing.
:::

## Summary and Next Steps

<InArticleAd />


### Key concepts

1. **Bonded materials share the same strain**, so the stiffer one carries $n$ times the stress at the same distance from the neutral axis.
2. **The transformed section** draws the other material $n = E_{other}/E_{reference}$ times as wide. Heights do not change.
3. **The neutral axis** is the centroid of the transformed section.
4. **The stress** is $My/I$ in the reference material and $n \times My/I$ in the other.
